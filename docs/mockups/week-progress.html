Pace · GNOME dropdown and dashboard

Week progress: four directions

The decision: which shape the week element takes in the dropdown (and, in the same style, the dashboard card), and whether it takes the week’s verdict colour. Every slice is drawn at the real dropdown width (340 px, dark Shell card) with the design’s example numbers from B1–B6. Each direction has to answer two questions in one glance: how far am I and how close is the pay line, without the three cramped ticks.

What it gets wrong
8 px of saturated green is the loudest thing in the menu on a normal day. The track runs to 45 h (“expected”), so the three ticks land inside the last 9 % of it, 11 px apart, and need a legend row to be read at all. The legend repeats numbers the user already knows.
What every direction below keeps
The track ends at the target (42.5 h), not at 45. “Expected 45” is a Settings fact, not a weekly question. The only line that matters mid-week is the pay line (41 h), and the hours-to-pay number is always printed, never only drawn.
Palette (B1)
green: on track, ahead, done amber: tight, behind, short red: pay risk, LOP blue: bill your hours, today grey: offline, leave

A · Thin line and a caption

Communicates
Progress as a 3 px line to the target, one 2 px notch at the pay line, and a caption that prints the two gaps (“10.9 h to pay line · 12.4 to target”). The notch is a landmark; the caption is the answer.
Colour rule
Line takes the verdict colour every time (same palette as the top-bar dot). The notch is white at 45 % until pay is in play, then red.
Trade-off
Quietest and closest to what the dashboard already moved to. But the 41 → 42.5 gap is 3.5 % of the track (11 px), so between SHORT and DONE the line alone cannot tell you which side of the pay line you are on; the caption has to.

B · Segmented five-day bar

Communicates
One cell per working day, filled to that day’s share of the target (8.5 h). The shape of the week is visible: where hours went missing, which day is today (blue outline), which day is not billed yet (dashed blue), leave (hatched). The pay line is a notch 1.5 h before the end of the last cell. This bar can replace the five day chips underneath, saving a row.
Colour rule
Only on risk. Billed days are neutral white; a short day is red; the notch turns red when pay is in play. Blue marks today and “not billed yet”, as it does in the chips now.
Trade-off
Richest picture, least arithmetic. The notch reads as “a position on Friday”, which is only true when every day is about 8.5 h; a 9.6-h Monday is capped at a full cell, so the bar under-reports AHEAD. The caption carries the real numbers.

C · Number first, no bar

Communicates
The hours as a 28 px figure with a 10 px verdict dot, then two text lines: the gaps (“10.9 h to pay line · 12.4 h to target”) and the pace (“needs 8.0 h/day · 4.5 h left today”). Nothing to decode.
Colour rule
The dot only. Text stays white; the gap line goes red when pay is in play, blue for BILL, green for DONE.
Trade-off
Most honest: a bar in which 41 and 42.5 are 11 px apart was never going to show the pay margin. But there is no “how far along” feel; 30.1 needs a moment of mental division, and the day chips below carry all of the shape.

D · Runway with a buffer band

Communicates
A 6 px track to the target whose last 1.5 h (41 → 42.5) is a lighter band: the “short but pay safe” zone the rules actually define. Fill reaching the band = pay safe; fill covering it = done. No tick, no legend: the zone is the pay line. BILL shows the unbilled day as a dashed ghost ahead of the fill, so “bill Fri and the week is done” is drawn, not said.
Colour rule
Monochrome fill (white at 82 %) on a normal week; red only for PAY RISK and LOP; green only for DONE; grey when offline. The last-day gap keeps a white fill (the verdict is still ON TRACK, as in B1) and shows the gap in a red band edge and caption. The verdict colour otherwise lives in the title and the top-bar dot.
Trade-off
The band is a convention the user learns once (a tooltip on first hover covers it). On a 340 px menu it is 11 px wide, enough to see but not to read a number off; the caption still prints the gap.

Edge cases

Every direction on the awkward weeks: a long day covering a short one, a flat week that still loses a day, half days, holidays, a weekend rescue, overtime, a correction that lowers the total, and a week that has not been billed at all. Hours per day are labelled above each row; the HR line is the rule from “Pace rules v4”: d = working days after leave and holidays, pay line = 9·(⌈d⌉−1)+5, target = max(8.5·d, pay line), credited days = ⌊total/9⌋ + (remainder ≥ 5 ? 1 : 0), LOP = d − credited. A zero-hour day is “not billed yet” from 20:00 until 12:00 the next working day, then a shortfall.

Colour rule, isolated

Shape and colour are independent choices. Direction D under the three rules, on the four stages where they differ most. (A takes the same three rules the same way; B and C only have the “risk” and “dot” variants shown above.)

By state: fill always takes the verdict colour
Only on risk: white fill, red when pay is in play, green when done
Never: element is monochrome, colour lives in the title and top-bar dot only

Recommendation

D, with colour only on risk. The band answers “how close to the pay line” without a tick or a legend and matches how the rules work (41–42.5 is a zone, not a point), while a white fill keeps a normal week quiet. Red then appears only when pay is actually in play, which is the one moment this element must be loud, and the verdict colour stays where it already is: the top-bar dot and the title.

The edge cases do not change the shape choice, but they sharpen it: in seven of the twelve awkward weeks (1, 2, 6, 7, 12 and partly 5, 11) what the user needs is which day, and that is exactly what the five day chips under the bar already carry. So the recommendation is D plus the existing day chips, with short days red and pending days dashed in the chips as they are now. The three cases a bar cannot express (half-day line stays 41, overflow size, a correction) are handled by D’s caption and ghost, not by drawing. If the chips are ever dropped to save a row, switch the bar to B.

Second choice: A with the same colour rule and the same chips.
