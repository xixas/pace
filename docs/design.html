Always know if your billed hours are on pace

A small Linux app that always shows the person their week (billed hours against the 8.5 h/day target and the loss-of-pay line) and their month (per-project targets like Tweelin 140 h) in the panel. It reads hours from PineStem and leave and holidays from DhiHRMS, all through hrms-api. It nudges them while a shortfall is still fixable.

Today

The pinestem-billing-report skill runs on demand, for Vikas only. It ignores leave and holidays and hard-codes 42.5 h.

Target

A Linux user installs one package and signs in once with Microsoft. From then on the panel shows 30.1 / 42.5 h in a colour that means what HR means, and a nudge arrives before pay is at risk.

Done when

Vikas on GNOME and one colleague on KDE see week and month numbers that match the HR billing report to 0.01 h, with leave applied. Pace never shows green in a week HR docks pay.

What changed since draft 1

Draft 2 is Linux-only, and Fable reviewed it in two ways. A pre-mortem (assume Pace failed in three months and ask why) checked the design. Four rounds of timeline simulation replayed 18 realistic weeks poll by poll and judged each verdict from the employee's side. Every finding below is either fixed in this draft or listed under Risks.

ChangeWhy

Architecture: a quiet daemon, windows on demand

hrms-api is still the only door. It already calls PineStem with org credentials and maps each employee (common/pinestem/pinestem.client.ts:185), and it owns the leave and holiday calendar (common/calendar/working-calendar.ts). On the desktop, Pace splits into a small headless daemon (always running, no GTK loaded) and a GTK4 UI process that starts only when you open a window. That keeps the idle footprint low without needing a webview.

New: my-hours

?from=2026-10-26&to=2026-11-01 returns one row per day per project: {date, project_code, project_name, billable_min, non_billable_min}. It is built on getEmployeeDetailedBilling and aggregated by day on the server.

New: my-days

The output of working-calendar.ts for a range: off days, holidays, opted-in restricted holidays only, approved leave with half-days as 0.5, comp-off and joining date. It mirrors what leaveCounts feeds the LOP report.

Speed and memory budget

Number in the panel < 300 ms after login (cached status.json). paced idles < 35 MB with no GTK loaded. A UI window runs about 60–80 MB while open and frees it on close. CPU stays near 0 between polls.

Pace rules v4: pay first, then target

The week has two separate questions. Will HR dock pay? HR counts floor(total/9) + (remainder ≥ 5) days against working days, weekly, on billable + non-billable hours. Will I hit 8.5 h/day? The colour answers the pay question first. Late billing is treated as "not billed yet" rather than a shortfall, and the last working day always shows the hard gap.

Inputs

d = working days after leave/holidays (0.5 steps)
lop = 9·(⌈d⌉−1) + 5   // 41 h for 5 or 4.5 days
target = max(8.5·d, lop)
pending = 8.5 for each zero-hour working day, from its 20:00
  until 12:00 next working day (Fri → Mon 12:00)
eff = logged + pending
need/day = (target − eff_before_today) ÷ days left incl. today
  // steady all day; today's own progress shows as "x h left today"
after 20:00 today counts as past

Verdict, first match wins
DONElogged ≥ target
BILLonly "not billed yet" days stand between you and DONE
PAY RISKlop out of reach at 10.5 h/day, or the day is over under it. Names the short days.
SHORTno days left, at or above lop: pay safe
BEHINDneed/day > 10.5, pay still reachable
TIGHT9.0 < need/day ≤ 10.5
AHEAD≥ 9 h per past day; never on the last working day
ON TRACKotherwise

On the last working day under the LOP line, the label adds · pay needs N h. After the week ends: DONE, SHORT (pay safe) or LOP n days, computed exactly as HR does.

Month, per project

Billable minutes only. The lead sets each target as fixed (a client commitment, the default) or prorated: eff_target = target × avail_days ÷ month_working_days. Then need/day = (eff_target − billed) ÷ remaining avail_days, with the same bands scaled by that project's share of the day.

Still open (hrms-api owner)

Do Saturday billings count in HR's weekly window? The window comes from the caller's fromDate/toDate (billing-report.xlsx.ts:78-79). Until that's answered, Pace ignores weekend billings for pay, so it can only under-promise. Simulated week 10 (8 h × 5, then 3 h Saturday) shows LOP 1 day, not DONE. Is a half-day leave 0.5 in leave.total?

Build order

The app builds in eight slices, each with a check that proves it done. Slice 1 runs in hrms-api's own repo through its own session, and only on your go typed there. Slices 2–3 can start before it lands by running against recorded fixtures (saved API responses).

#SliceDone when
1hrms-api: my-hours + my-days, a 60 s cache and ?fresh=1 (TDD, in its repo)For a fixed week, my-hours totals equal the HR billing report's totals. my-days returns 0.5 for a half-day and excludes restricted holidays the person didn't opt into.
2Pace engine (pure Python), ported from sim/week-rules-v4.jspytest replays all 18 simulated weeks and matches week-rules-v4-out.txt cell for cell on verdicts and need/day (display rounds minutes half-up, so 267 min shows 4.5 h where the sim prints 4.4), plus a Monday 09:00 poll while last Friday is still "not billed yet". Fable judges any new timeline before a rule changes.
3paced: sign-in (PKCE, loopback), Secret Service token, poll, status.jsonOn Nobara the number reaches status.json within 300 ms of login. ps shows paced under 35 MB.
4GNOME extension pace@osmosys (forked from claude-usage)All 13 label states render from fixture status.json files. Screenshots are reviewed by Fable.
5Tray icon (StatusNotifierItem) + notificationsA KDE Plasma 6 VM and a COSMIC VM both show the icon. Left-click opens the mini widget, and right-click shows the text menu (a tray icon gets nothing richer).
6GTK4 UI: sign-in, targets, dashboard, settings, mini widgetEvery mock in sections A–C is rendered on Broadway (GTK's HTML5 display backend, used for headless screenshots) and looked at, and Fable reviews the PNGs.
7Packaging: .rpm, .deb, AppImage from GitHub ActionsA clean Fedora 44 and an Ubuntu 24.04 container install it and start paced.
8Pilot: Vikas + 2 colleagues for two weeksPace's verdict matched HR's LOP email every week. No colleague turned nudges off.

Later, not in v1: the my-targets endpoint plus the hrms-portal targets page (mock C9), Flathub, and Windows/macOS (with this toolkit that means new UI screens; the engine and status.json contract carry over).

Risks: Fable's pre-mortem, and the answer to each

If Pace fails, it will be because…What this draft does
Numbers look stale right after billing, so people stop trusting themThe server cache drops to 60 s. Refresh sends ?fresh=1 (limited to once a minute). Every surface shows "as of".
Green while HR docks payPay comes first in the rules, the half-day target rises to 41 h, and the last day always shows "pay needs N h". Simulation plus 4 Fable rounds; the pilot compares against the real LOP email.
"Always visible" fails on stock GNOME: no tray, and extensions break with each Shell releaseThe package ships the panel extension. Setup enables it, which needs one re-login (A10), and a version-skew banner covers upgrades (C10). Without the extension, notifications and the mini widget still work.
No keyring on COSMIC or minimal KDE, so a sign-in prompt every morningA one-time choice (A9): a file in ~/.local/share/pace with mode 600, like rclone.conf, or sign in each login.
It feels like surveillance: nightly nudges and locked targetsEvery nudge has its own switch plus "Snooze today", the data stays on the person's machine, and leads set targets but never see the app.
Wayland can't keep the widget on topThe widget behaves like a sticky note. KDE users can pin it with a window rule. The panel label is the always-visible surface.

Decisions

Each row has a recommendation. Row 3 (who owns targets) and row 9 (fixed vs prorated) are the ones I'd defend least. Pick per row and press Queue answers, or say "go" in the terminal.

#DecisionChoiceThe fact that decides it
1Where the app gets hours hrms-api already holds org PineStem credentials and the user mapping (pinestem.client.ts:185).
2Entra registration hrms-api checks only aud + access_as_user (microsoft-sso.service.ts:64-71). A separate app gets its own consent screen and its own revocation.
3Who owns targets Targets differ per person and that's the lead's call. A preset file needs no hrms-portal work for v1.
4Toolkit The first users are all on Linux. Tauri only imitates GTK in a webview (80–150 MB per window). Windows/macOS later means about 6 new screens; the engine carries over.
5Repo home Pace is Vikas's personal project (decided 2026-10-28). Private for now; packages shared by hand when needed.
6Packages The repo is private, so its releases are too: Vikas shares the files by hand for now. No COPR, no extensions.gnome.org. The GNOME extension ships inside the package.
7Week rules Draft 1 showed AHEAD at 40 h on a Friday that cost a day's pay. v4 came back clean from Fable on round 4.
8No keyring available Without it, COSMIC and minimal KDE users sign in every morning. It's the same trade-off rclone.conf already makes.
9Project targets during leave Prorating Tweelin 140 → 133 hides a real 7 h gap if 140 is the client's number.
10Name Short and verb-like, and it reads well in a tray tooltip.
Before slice 1: type your go in the hrms-api session's own window (a relayed go doesn't count). Ask its owner the two open questions: do Saturday hours count, and is a half-day 0.5 in leave.total? An Azure admin creates the "Pace" Entra registration.
